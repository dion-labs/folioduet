Who it may suit

For people who prefer more than one way into a text

Reading and listening together is sometimes called bimodal reading. FolioDuet provides the mechanics without making medical or comprehension guarantees.

  • Readers working through dense reports or academic material
  • Language learners following pronunciation in visible context
  • People who prefer multisensory focus for long passages
  • Writers reviewing pacing while keeping the draft on screen

Less window juggling

Before you begin

What the reader processes—and what it does not

  • A PDF must contain a usable text layer; built-in OCR for image-only scans is not promised.
  • Complex columns, headers, or unusual encoding can affect extracted reading order.
  • Neural narration is streamed, needs a connection, and does not create an MP3 or M4B download.
  • Word timing and voice availability can vary with the speech provider or browser fallback.

Your files are not all treated the same: the original PDF remains on the import device. A signed-in library can sync extracted text and progress. Neural TTS processes only the text selected for speech through the configured provider. Consent-gated analytics can be declined without disabling the reader.

Frequently asked questions

Reading and listening in sync

Does FolioDuet highlight every word as it reads?

The active-word highlight updates from timing supplied by the speech engine. Neural providers with alignment data offer the intended synchronized experience; timing precision can vary when playback falls back to a browser system voice.

Can I click a word or passage to start there?

Yes. Words in the FolioDuet reading layer are selectable playback starting points.

Will it keep turning pages?

Playback advances through readable text blocks and updates the current reader page. A page without usable extracted text may have nothing to narrate.

Can I change the voice and speed?

Yes. The reader includes speech-provider and voice settings, plus 0.8×, 1×, 1.2×, 1.5×, and 2× playback. Available voices depend on the provider or browser.

Does it work on phone and desktop?

FolioDuet has responsive phone and desktop layouts. Provider and browser voice support can differ between devices, so the exact set of voices is not universal.

Does it support image-only or scanned PDFs?

Not by itself. The PDF needs a usable text layer; FolioDuet does not currently promise built-in OCR for image-only scans.
